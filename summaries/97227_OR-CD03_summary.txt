### 4. Multnomah County, OR-CD03 (Zip: 97227)

**Complexity Rank: 98th Percentile**

The complexity of the Portland ballot is not just about the number of questions (27), but about the extreme number of candidates in its local races, presenting a significant challenge of choice overload for the voter.

The most unusual and demanding aspect is the implementation of a new voting system for the Portland City Council. Voters are asked to choose **three candidates for each of the four council districts**, with races that are flooded with dozens of options. The race for City Council District 3 is particularly daunting, with **30 candidates** vying for three seats. Districts 2 and 4 are similarly crowded with 22 and 30 candidates, respectively. The Mayoral race also features a packed field of **18 candidates**.

This structure requires an extraordinary amount of voter research. Without party labels to guide them, voters must attempt to differentiate between dozens of individuals in each race, many of whom have limited public profiles. This format, combined with 10 state and local ballot measures on topics ranging from ranked-choice voting to city charter amendments, creates a ballot that is not only long but also structurally complex. A voter must be prepared to spend considerable time researching the vast fields of candidates to make an informed choice, making this one of the most uniquely challenging ballots in the country.
